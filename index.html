How I Work

Four steps, same approach whether it's academic or shipping to production.

01

Frame the real question

Before testing, I pin down what's actually being evaluated and why it matters — a vague "improve usability" becomes a testable question tied to a specific behavior or decision.

02

Investigate with the right method

I choose between qualitative studies, heuristic evaluation, or both depending on the question — recruiting participants, designing tasks, adapting Nielsen's heuristics to context.

03

Separate signal from noise

Not all friction is a problem. I look for the type and source — operational barriers to remove versus deliberate friction worth preserving — before proposing a fix.

04

Turn findings into something buildable

Recommendations come with concrete artifacts — a redesigned flow, prioritized roadmap, or working mockup — not just a slide of insights.

Case Study 01

x-Plica

Cognitive friction and critical agency in hybrid human-AI workflows

Role: UX Researcher (solo) Method: Qualitative study, adapted Nielsen heuristics Publication: Revista Entretejidos, 2026

Context

x-Plica is a human-AI workflow (Grok 3 + NotebookLM) designed to help people critically analyze viral content on platform X, without handing the final judgment to the AI. I evaluated whether the system supported critical reasoning or introduced friction that interfered with trust and reflection.

My role and method

I designed and ran the full study: recruitment, task design, observation protocol, and qualitative analysis. I adapted four of Nielsen's heuristics to the human-AI context, pairing them with user observation to understand the experience as a whole.

Key findings

55.6%dropout rate due to operational friction (switching between 3 platforms)
4.5/5rating of the mind map (best-reviewed component)
3–5/5variance in how the risk semaphore was understood

The central finding: two types of friction with opposite effects. Operational friction (switching platforms) pushed out more than half the participants before they could finish the task, while cognitive friction in the analysis workflow helped improve critical reflection when well-scaffolded.

Design recommendations

  • Native integration (overlay/extension) to remove platform switching
  • Redesign the risk semaphore as sequential binary questions
  • 3-step conceptual onboarding with real examples from X
  • Selective activation to prevent alert fatigue

Case Study 02

Kroma QA Studio

Turning off "Hard Mode" in Game QA operations

Role: Diagnosis + solution design Status: Approved, rolling out in tiers

The problem

The QA team was operating in unnecessary hard mode: 35–40% of their time went into administrative tasks (spreadsheets, copy-paste, inconsistent formats) instead of real testing. The cause was a fragmented flow with multiple handoffs and unreliable metadata capture.

35–40%time spent on admin (current state)
15%target after optimization

The solution: Nielsen's heuristics applied to the ticket flow

Visibility of system status The active build is inherited and shown automatically on every ticket, no manual entry.
Recognition over recall A bank of technical synonyms as selectable chips (Freeze, Crash, Lag, Glitch…) instead of free-text fields.
Consistency and standards Unified dropdowns and a closed catalog of authorized builds, removing spelling variants.
Error prevention Flow segmented by bug lifecycle stage (Ticket → Verify Open → Verify Fix → NMI), each with specific fields.

Outcome

Project approved. Three-phase roadmap: quick wins (unify dropdowns, remove redundant fields), consolidation (automated forms, DRE measurement), and maturity (technical sync with development).
